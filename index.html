Leadership and people

  • New CEO
  • New chair or board changes

Marketing team and hiring

  • New CMO or marketing director
  • Marketing roles advertised
  • Brand, social or ecommerce leads hired
  • Marketing leader leaves
  • In-house team built up or cut
  • Procurement or roster roles advertised

Agency relationship

  • Same agency for three years or more
  • Key people leave the agency
  • Agency merged, sold or closed
  • A conflict from the agency's new client
  • Contract end dates in tenders and filings

Performance and strategy

  • Profit warning
  • Falling like-for-like sales
  • New strategy or turnaround plan
  • Store openings or closures
  • Entry to new markets

Brand and launches

  • Signs of a rebrand: trade marks, design roles
  • New product or range
  • New store format
  • New channel: grocery, direct, wholesale
  • New name or packaging

Media and spend

  • Ad spend up or down sharply
  • Shift between channels
  • Media agency moved
  • New sponsorships and partnerships

A way in that isn't another cold email

A new CMO is flooded with agency emails in her first month, and most go unanswered. So Felix doesn't stop at an opener. For every brand it plans the routes most likely to get you a conversation, in the right order.

  • Warm routes. Named people at your agency who know them, found in your own CRM, team inboxes and calendars, past clients and former colleagues.
  • Gatekeepers. Pitch consultants and advisers likely to shape the review.
  • Something on paper. Felix writes a one-page point of view and posts it for you: printed and sent, no trip to the post office.
  • Email, at the right moment. One of the routes, not the whole plan.
  • Reasons to stay in touch. Felix flags the next good moment for months, not once.
